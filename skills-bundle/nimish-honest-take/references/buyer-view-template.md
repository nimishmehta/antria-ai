# Buyer-view brief — template

Written in the first person *as the buyer persona*. Executive, blunt, value-first. The question behind every line: **would I actually pay for this, and why or why not?** Target length: one page, scannable in two minutes.

---

## Title
`<Product> — A Buyer's Honest Take` (with a one-line persona credential, e.g. "From the desk of a CFO who's signed ten renewal checks and switched brokers twice.")

## 1. The job I'm hiring this for
The real problem on my desk, stated in my words — independent of this product. What I'm actually trying to fix or avoid. One short paragraph.

## 2. My pains, ranked
A table — the heart of the buyer view:

| Pain | Severity (1–5) | $ I'd attach | My alternative today |
|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | (status quo / incumbent / do-nothing) |

Rank by severity × dollars. This is *my* ranking, from *my* world — not derived from what the product does.

## 3. What this actually does for me
Only what's genuinely built (from the ledger). For each: which of my ranked pains it touches, and how much of that pain it actually removes. Name what's real value to me.

## 4. Where it falls short
The gaps that matter *to me*: pains it doesn't touch, the headline it promises but doesn't deliver, the switching cost, the trust/integration/compliance worries. Be specific.

## 5. Would I buy it?
- **Verdict:** yes / no / not yet.
- **At what price** — and against which alternative.
- **The deal-breaker** — the one thing that decides it.
- **What would make me sign** — the shortest path to a yes.

## One-line verdict
The whole thing in a sentence, in my voice.

---

### Rules
- Compare to a real alternative on every pain — buyers never evaluate in a vacuum.
- Don't credit promised/mock features; I can't buy a roadmap.
- If it genuinely solves my top pain at a good price, say yes clearly. Honesty cuts both ways.
