# Seller-view brief — template

Written in the first person *as the operator persona* — a veteran who has built/sold/run businesses in this space. Executive, blunt, value-first. The question behind every line: **does this win deals and keep them, and is it defensible?** Target length: one to two pages, scannable.

---

## Title
`<Product> — A Operator's Honest Teardown` (with a one-line persona credential, e.g. "From someone who's carried both a 150-account book and a 9,000-seat enterprise deal.")

## 1. The one-line verdict
Lead with it. What this really is, stripped of the pitch, in a sentence. (e.g., "Roughly one real product, one commodity giveaway, and one consumer experiment.")

## 2. What this really is
The honest framing of the whole offering. What job it's truly doing in the market vs. what the deck says.

## 3. Built vs. claimed
The gap, plainly. The headline promise vs. what the code/feature actually does today. This is where deals are won or lost on the reference call.

## 4. Component-by-component
For each meaningful piece — a card or row each:
- **What it is** (one line)
- **Where it falls** — which buyer job it serves, and the gap
- **Need or nice?** — does it kill a top pain, or is it a peripheral nice-to-have?
- **Competitive reality** — the named incumbents/alternatives, and whether this beats them or is strictly weaker
- **Verdict** — keep / fold-in / bundle-don't-build / reposition / cut

## 5. How many products is this, really?
The consolidation answer: one, two, or N. Where the redundancies are (duplicate logins, duplicate assistants, overlapping surfaces). What belongs as a module vs. a standalone vs. an orphan.

## 6. Build-vs-buy
For each non-core piece: build, bundle a proven partner, or integrate? Where is in-house engineering being wasted reinventing a commodity?

## 7. Differentiation & moat — honestly
What's actually defensible vs. easily copied. Is the wedge real, or is it table stakes dressed up?

## 8. What's missing to win and retain
The load-bearing pieces not yet built that would actually move the deal and the renewal — in priority order.

## One-line verdict
The strategy/build call in a sentence (e.g., "Strategy right, build aimed at the wrong half of the problem").

---

### Rules
- Name real competitors and the real economics. No generic market-speak.
- Credit genuine assets before swinging — it's what makes the teardown trustworthy.
- Separate "real product" from "feature" from "marketing asset." Don't let a pitch call three things a platform when it's one.
