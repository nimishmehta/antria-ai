# Hiring Kit

Four artifacts that make hiring consistent and fair, so decisions rest on evidence not vibes.

## 1 · Job description
Sell the role, don't list demands.
- **What this person will achieve** in 6–12 months (outcomes), not a 20-line skills laundry list.
- Who they work with, what success looks like, why the role matters now.
- Must-haves kept to the genuine few; everything else is "nice to have."
- Honest about stage, pay range if the founder wants, and what's hard about the job.

## 2 · Interview scorecard
A **rubric**, not a feeling.
- 4–6 competencies tied to the role's outcomes.
- Each scored on a defined scale (e.g. 1–4 with what each level means).
- Space for evidence per competency. A clear hire/no-hire recommendation field.

## 3 · Screening questions
- A short set that quickly separates fit from no-fit, mapped to the must-haves.
- Include what a strong vs weak answer sounds like, so a non-expert can judge.

## 4 · Candidate summary
- After an interview: strengths, concerns, evidence, and a clear recommendation against the scorecard. One page.

## Concrete example (JD outcome line)
Weak: "5+ years experience, proficient in X, Y, Z, strong communicator, team player."
Strong: "In your first 6 months you'll own our onboarding funnel and lift activation from 40% to 60%. You've done conversion work like this before and can talk through how."

## Quality bar (every time)
- **JD leads with outcomes**, must-haves are few and real.
- **Scorecard is a rubric** — defined levels, competencies tied to the role, evidence captured.
- Screening questions come with what good/weak answers look like.
- Candidate summary ends in a clear, evidence-backed recommendation.
