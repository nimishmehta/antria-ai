YOUR SIDE · about 2 minutes

How you ship an update

Every time you want to release a change:

  1. Change the version number (e.g. 0.3.1 → 0.3.2) — this is the flag that says "something new."
  2. Run one build command. It packages the new Mac and Windows versions automatically.
  3. Publish it. The new version lands on GitHub Releases — a click, or fully automatic. That's you done.

You never touch your users. You just put the new version "on the shelf," once.

YOUR USER'S SIDE · zero effort

How a user receives it

They do nothing at all. Behind the scenes:

  1. Their app quietly checks GitHub each time they open it — "is there a newer version?"
  2. If yes, it downloads the update in the background while they keep working. No pop-up, no waiting.
  3. Next time they close and reopen the app, it's already the new version. Done — silently.

Works the same on Mac and Windows. If the internet's down, the app just runs as normal and checks again later.

The one-line version

You put the new version on a shelf (GitHub). Every user's app walks past that shelf on startup, sees a newer box, and swaps itself for it — without bothering anyone.

How fast do users get it?

Usually within a launch or two after you publish — most people restart their app within a day.

Can it break someone's app mid-use?

No. The swap only happens when they close the app, never while they're working.

Do I have to notify anyone?

No. Publishing is the whole job. The apps handle the rest themselves.
